Folding expandable houses and flat pack (knock-down) houses are the two most common prefab systems for camps, offices and temporary housing — but they are engineered for different priorities. Choosing the right one saves real money.

## Folding expandable: speed and space on site

A folding house ships as a standard container and expands on site with a crane, roughly doubling its floor area in a few hours. You get a wide, finished, pre-wired room almost immediately. The trade-off: each unit is a single container in transit, so per-unit freight is higher than flat pack, and expansion needs crane access.

## Flat pack: lowest freight, most flexibility

Flat pack houses ship as panels and frames, packing several units into one 40ft container. Assembly needs simple tools and a small crew, and units can be reconfigured, extended or relocated project after project. The trade-off: on-site assembly takes longer than a crane-erected folding unit, so labor planning matters.

## How to decide

Choose folding when: you need a finished room fast, crane access is available, and each location justifies the higher transport cost. Choose flat pack when: freight cost dominates (large quantities, long distances), the site will change over time, or local crews will assemble. Many large contractors run both systems — folding for quick response units, flat pack for volume camps.

## A hybrid reality

The best projects treat the two systems as a toolkit. Site camps often combine flat pack dormitory blocks with folding offices and clinics that deploy in hours. Tell your supplier the project profile — quantities, distance, crane availability, climate — and let them propose the mix.
